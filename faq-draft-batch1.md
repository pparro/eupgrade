- **[Community]** — widely reported by experienced flyers, not officially published by AC;

## Basics

**What's an eUpgrade, in one line?**
[Official] A credit-based way to move from Economy into Premium Economy or Business on a
flight you've already booked, without buying a new ticket.

**Does it happen automatically?**
[Official] No. You have to actively request it — either in the app or by phone — and you
need enough valid credits to cover the route. If you never request one, Air Canada is
perfectly happy to fly the seat up front empty rather than move you into it.

**How many credits will my specific flight cost?**
[Official] It depends on three things: how far you're flying, which fare brand you booked,
and which cabin you're moving into. Rather than memorize the chart, use the calculator on
this site — it's built directly from Air Canada's published tables.

**I'm flying somewhere with a connection — is the cost calculated leg by leg, or for the
whole trip?**
[Official] As long as your layover is under 24 hours, it's priced as one continuous journey,
not as separate segments. Occasionally this means a short connecting leg costs nothing extra
on top of the long-haul segment. One catch: each leg still has to clear its own upgrade
separately — if only one segment has room, that's the only one that upgrades, and you're
only charged credits for the leg that actually cleared.

**Can I request Business on one leg and Premium Economy on another?**
[Official] No — you pick one target cabin for the trip. If you request Business, you're
automatically in line for Premium Economy too, as a fallback if Business doesn't clear.

**I already used a bid upgrade or last-minute upgrade into Premium Economy. Can I now use
eUpgrade credits to try for Business?**
[Official] No — only one type of upgrade mechanism applies per segment. Once you've
upgraded a leg by any method, eUpgrades can't stack on top of it.

**Once my eUpgrade clears, can I lose the seat?**
[Official] Very rarely. Simply having lower status than someone who requests later doesn't
bump you. The exception is operational — an aircraft swap to a plane with a smaller (or no)
Business cabin can force a downgrade. [Community] Reports suggest Air Canada treats this as
"you flew the cabin you paid for" and doesn't typically offer meaningful compensation, since
technically nothing beyond the original economy ticket was guaranteed.

## Timing & the upgrade list

**When can I even request an upgrade?**
[Official] The moment you book, for almost everyone. The one exception: if you're upgrading
a travel companion who isn't on your booking, that request can only be made at the airport
once check-in opens for the flight.

**When can it actually confirm — not just be requested, but clear?**
[Official] That depends on your fare and your status, and it's the single most
misunderstood part of the whole system:
- **Flexible fares** (Latitude, Premium Economy Flexible) can clear the moment you book —
  no waiting window at all, if there's space.
- **Everything else** has to wait for your personal clearance window to open — a set number
  of days before departure that gets earlier the higher your status is, typically 3–14 days
  depending on route, fare, and tier.
- **No status at all?** You can only request on a flexible fare, and only starting 7 days out.

Use the calculator on this site to see your exact window for a given status/route/fare
combination.

**What does it mean when people talk about "R space"?**
[Community] R is the internal booking-class letter Air Canada uses for Business eUpgrade
inventory — separate from the seats sold for cash. If R shows greater than zero, there's
room for upgrades to clear in advance; if R is at zero, anyone requesting gets waitlisted.
For Premium Economy, the equivalent letter is N. Air Canada doesn't guarantee this space
exists on every flight — sometimes they decide months out that a plane will sell out on its
own and release no upgrade space at all.

**Can I check this R/N space myself?**
[Community] Only through a paid fare-availability tool, since it isn't shown anywhere in the
consumer booking flow. And timing matters: on a flexible fare, checking early can be useful,
since your upgrade could clear immediately if space exists. On a non-flexible fare, checking
before your clearance window opens is generally a waste of time — nothing can happen until
the window opens regardless of space.

**Is the "will this clear" indicator in the app or website reliable?**
[Community] Not especially. It's been widely reported to say "confirmed" when a request
later gets waitlisted, and the reverse — waitlisted flights that actually clear fine. The one
thing it's fairly reliable about: if you're outside your clearance window, it will correctly
tell you that you'll be waitlisted.

**Should I trust the seat map to judge my odds?**
[Community] No — and this is worth repeating because it's the most common mistake. A cabin
that looks half-empty on the seat map can be fully sold to people who simply haven't picked
seats. A cabin that looks full can still have upgrade room behind the scenes, or show blocked
seats that are really just waitlisted requests. The seat map is not a reliable signal either
way.

**What happens if I'm still waitlisted close to departure?**
[Official] Once you're inside 36 hours of departure, nothing more can clear in advance —
everything left on the list gets settled at the airport instead.

**When exactly does that happen at the airport?**
[Official] Check-in closes 60 minutes before departure (this is now the same cutoff for
domestic and international flights, following a 2024 change). About five minutes later —
roughly 55 minutes before departure — the upgrade list is processed at the gate, in priority
order.

**Once I've boarded, is it over?**
[Community] Functionally, yes, in almost every case. Once you're marked boarded, you drop
off the visible upgrade list, and while there are scattered reports of a proactive agent
upgrading someone after boarding, it isn't a normal or reliable outcome. If you're genuinely
hoping to clear, it's better to hang back near the gate than to board early.

**How is the upgrade list actually ordered — what decides who clears first?**
[Official/Community — mechanism confirmed by AC's published priority rules, sub-tier detail
from community reporting]
1. **Cabin first:** anyone already ticketed in Premium Economy outranks anyone coming from
   Economy.
2. **Status next**, and this is the main event — from top-tier elite status down through
   each lower tier in order.
3. **Fare brand**, as a tiebreaker within the same status level — a more flexible, pricier
   fare beats a cheaper one.
4. **Check-in time**, as the final tiebreaker when everything else is equal.

[Community] Within elite status itself there are reported sub-tiers — for instance,
million-miler recognition and holding a premium Aeroplan credit card can each nudge you
ahead of someone at the identical elite tier without those distinctions. None of this is
officially published; it's inferred from patterns on the public upgrade list over time.

**Can I actually see where I rank?**
[Official] Yes — once check-in opens, the Air Canada app shows an upgrade list for the
flight. [Community] Worth knowing: that list isn't exclusively eUpgrade requests — it also
includes other standby and upgrade categories, and it's occasionally been reported as
incomplete or out of order.
