## Earning & validity

**How long do eUpgrade credits last before they expire?**
[Official] 12 months from the day they're deposited into your account — or 24 months if you
hold a premium Aeroplan credit card. The card doesn't give you extra credits, just extra time
to use the ones you have.

**What's the actual cutoff on the expiry date?**
[Official] Your flight has to depart before 11:59pm local time (local to wherever the flight
is departing from) on the day your credits expire. A flight departing after midnight on
expiry day is too late.

**Can I push my expiry date out further?**
[Official] Indirectly, yes. If your benefits package lets you choose *when* in the year to
claim your credits, claiming them later gives you a later expiry date on that batch.

**If I have credits with different expiry dates, which ones get used first?**
[Official] Whichever are expiring soonest. This is handled automatically after the flight —
so if you had a request tied up for a future trip that ultimately doesn't use those credits
(say the upgrade never clears, or you cancel), the soonest-expiring credits may end up
released back to your account rather than the ones you'd expect.

**Can I just buy more eUpgrade credits?**
[Official] No. They come only from status benefits or hitting mileage/SQC thresholds — there's
no purchase option.

## Upgrading someone else

**Can I use my credits to upgrade another passenger?**
[Official] Depends on the relationship:
- **Same booking as you (max 3 people per booking):** you can request it any time, the
  normal way. If your booking has more than three passengers, none of them are eligible for
  advance upgrade requests — only day-of.
- **Different booking, same flight ("travel companion"):** only requestable at the airport,
  on the day of travel. [Official] Super Elite members get a shortcut here — a concierge can
  submit this request by phone or email once check-in opens, without needing to be at the
  airport.
- **Different booking, different flight:** not possible for most members. Super Elites are
  the exception — once a year, they can name one "nominee" who can be upgraded on any flight
  that person is on.

**What's the most people I can upgrade at once?**
[Official] Five total (yourself plus two people on your booking plus two travel companions)
for most members. Super Elite members get a sixth slot for their nominee.

**Does everyone on a shared booking need space to open up at once, or can they clear one at
a time?**
[Official] Before the gate, everyone on the same booking needs seats to open simultaneously —
if two of you are booked together, there have to be two seats. At the gate, it's processed
in priority order and your group can end up split between cabins, or one person clearing
while another doesn't — if that outcome would bother you, you can ask the gate agent to pull
your whole party off the list rather than risk a split.

## Money

**When do my credits actually get taken from my account — at request, at clearance, or after
the flight?**
[Official] Immediately at the moment you request the upgrade, regardless of whether it ever
clears.

**If it never clears, do I get the credits back?**
[Official] Yes — automatically, shortly after the flight, whether the upgrade simply didn't
clear or the flight itself was cancelled.

**What's the "add-on" fee some upgrades show?**
[Official] A cash charge on top of your credits, required on certain fare/route
combinations — mainly cheaper, less flexible fares on longer routes. The calculator on this
site shows you exactly when this applies.

**Is there any way around the add-on?**
[Official] Super Elite members are exempt on international flights — with one exception,
Standard-brand fares, where the add-on still applies even for Super Elite. There's no other
waiver. One nuance worth knowing: the exemption is based on *who submits the request*, not
who's flying. If a Super Elite requests the upgrade for their whole party, everyone's add-on
is waived — but if a non-Super-Elite requests it, the fee applies even if a Super Elite is
one of the people being upgraded.

**When does the add-on actually get charged?**
[Official] Only once the upgrade clears — not at the time you request it. Air Canada may
place a temporary hold on the card before that, though.

**If my upgrade clears and then I cancel the trip, do I get the add-on back?**
[Official] Only if Air Canada cancels or downgrades you. If you're the one who cancels, the
add-on fee generally isn't refunded.

**Do add-on fees count toward Status Qualifying Dollars?**
[Official] Yes, whether paid in cash or points, and it posts as its own separate transaction —
don't be surprised if it shows up at a different time than the SQD from the flight itself.

**If I upgraded someone else using my own credits, who earns the mileage/status credit?**
[Official] The person who actually flew in the upgraded seat — not whoever spent the credits
or paid the add-on.

## A genuinely obscure but useful edge case

**I'm flying one of Air Canada's "direct" flights with a stop (like YYZ–YVR–SYD as AC33, or
similar routings through YUL, GRU, or LHR). Does anything work differently?**
[Community] Yes, and it trips people up. These flights carry one flight number for the whole
journey but physically stop to refuel and swap some passengers partway. If you boarded at the
origin (say YYZ on AC33), you outrank anyone joining at the stopover — but only if there's
upgrade space on *both* legs. If the first leg has no space, you can't upgrade just the
second leg either, even if it has room, because once you've taken off you're no longer
eligible to be re-processed for a flight you're already on. If you boarded at the stopover
itself, normal rules apply as if that were your origin.

## Partner airlines

**Does crediting my mileage to a different airline's program stop me from using eUpgrade
credits?**
[Official] No — what matters is which Aeroplan number is on the reservation, not where
you're crediting the miles.

**Can I use eUpgrade credits on a partner airline's metal — United, Lufthansa, and so on?**
[Official] No. Only on flights physically operated by Air Canada.

**What about an Air Canada–operated flight I booked through a partner airline?**
[Official] If it's a cash ticket, generally yes, though the process differs: if it's a true
codeshare (marketed under the partner's flight number but flown by AC), you can only request
the upgrade on the day of travel, directly with an Air Canada agent. If it isn't a codeshare,
you can request it the normal way in advance. If it's a points ticket booked through another
airline's program, it can't be upgraded with eUpgrade credits at all.

## Not the same thing

eUpgrades are one of several ways to move into a better cabin on Air Canada — worth knowing
they're distinct mechanisms with their own rules, not different names for the same thing:
**Bid Upgrade** (paying miles or cash to bid for an upgrade), **Last-Minute Upgrades** (a
straight purchase at check-in), and **operational upgrades** (Air Canada's own discretionary
moves, unrelated to anything a passenger requests). This resource covers eUpgrades only.
